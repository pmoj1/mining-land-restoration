# Perseus annual-report recurrence review

Reviewed 22 October 2026 (Australia/Perth).

## Scope and method

Retrieved all 23 annual reports from 2004 to 2026. Reviewed decision-relevant management, operations, risk, rehabilitation and auditor sections. This is a thematic review, not a line-by-line financial audit or proof of a software gap. Early exploration/development years are not comparable operating baselines. Selected PDF pages are one-based physical page numbers; a PDF page can contain two printed pages. The manifest records source URLs, page counts and file hashes. The downloaded FY2005 report has no extractable text; a relevant rendered page and readable alternate archive text were reviewed. FY2020 auditor pages were also inspected as images.

## Findings and decision

1. Rehabilitation estimation/assurance is a recurring task: closure/rehabilitation provisions are key audit matters in all six reports FY2021–FY2026. Audit procedures repeatedly compare assumptions, rates and disturbance areas with mine plans and supporting evidence. This supports the proposed workflow's relevance, but does not establish repeated errors, underprovisioning, stale models, excessive staff time or willingness to buy software.
2. A broader recurring operating challenge is converting planned ore into predictable production and cost: equipment/power downtime, changing ore properties and delayed access recur in different years. These causes are different; grouping them does not establish one root cause or that one AI product can fix them.
3. Land access and approvals have repeated consequences, including exploration access in FY2005, operating-area access in FY2015–16, and Nkosuo negotiations in FY2025. An AI tracker cannot secure government decisions or community consent.
4. Weather relevance is supported by actual events, including Sissingué in FY2024. A specific inventory/ETA software deficiency is not demonstrated. FY2026 notes adverse wet-season effects at Yaouré but its climate disclosure identifies no material current-period weather-related operations/supply-chain impact; do not turn these disclosures into an unsupported loss estimate.
5. Responses and better years matter: power self-sufficiency/plant work in FY2015–17, better Edikan performance in FY2018 and FY2023, and improved Yaouré mining movements in FY2025 prevent a claim of continuous identical failure.

## Verdict on earlier ideas

- AI closure-cost assurance: supported as a recurring, material assurance workflow. The claimed mismatch/problem frequency and commercial gap remain unproven.
- Critical-supplies exception brief: weather/continuity rationale has support, but annual reports do not establish a recurring consumables stockout/ETA information problem.
- Original Australian rail/weather prototype: these African-mine reports do not establish customer fit for its Australian corridor coverage.
- Potential further discovery: explain which mine-plan assumptions become invalid as access, ore characteristics, contractor performance and plant availability change. This is an interview hypothesis, not a newly verified product category or approved roadmap.

## Comparable rehabilitation provision figures

US$ million: FY2023 42.483 (restated comparative in FY2024); FY2024 41.169; FY2025 48.484; FY2026 60.107. FY2023's original A$63.867m must not be compared directly to later USD values. Provision movements reflect development, cost expectations, discounting and other recognised changes, and are not evidence of avoidable waste or errors. FY2026 movement reconciles: 48.484 + 10.905 - 1.435 + 2.153 = 60.107. FY2024 provision decreased; do not describe the whole series as continually increasing.

## Limits

Annual reports are management-selected public disclosures, not a full incident register or internal software inventory. Repetition demonstrates recurring exposure or work, not customer dissatisfaction. No causal savings, software addressable market, novelty or venture-investment conclusion is established by this review. No implementation or outreach was performed.
